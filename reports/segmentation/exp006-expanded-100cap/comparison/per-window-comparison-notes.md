**Windows favoring DINOv2 (carrot, book, frisbee, toyplane, mouse) tell one
consistent, clear story:** in every single one, CUT3R-trained's prediction is
a large, diffuse, scattered blob that spills far past the true object into
the background — sometimes covering most of the frame (carrot, book,
toyplane) — while DINOv2 stays tight and close to the ground truth. This
is the over-guessing-foreground pattern the precision/recall numbers already
pointed to (CUT3R-trained recall 0.941 vs precision 0.786), now visible
directly: CUT3R is not just imprecise at the boundary, it is confidently
wrong over large areas of background.

**Windows favoring CUT3R-trained (apple, microwave, bench, 2x baseballbat)
are more mixed — DINOv2 does not fail the same way each time:**

- **apple (CUT3R 0.81 vs DINOv2 0.14):** DINOv2 mislocates entirely — two
  large blobs, neither overlapping the actual (small) apple on a visually
  busy board-game background. CUT3R's blob is small and roughly on-target.
- **microwave (0.85 vs 0.49) and bench (0.94 vs 0.69):** DINOv2 does **not**
  scatter noise here — its blob is in the right place, just noticeably
  smaller than the true extent (under-covers the object). CUT3R's blob
  matches the true extent more closely. A coverage/recall gap for DINOv2 on
  these two, not a mislocation or a noise problem.
- **both baseballbat windows (0.57 vs 0.32, and 0.89 vs 0.68):** here DINOv2
  is the one producing scattered, speckled noise across the frame (small
  disconnected red patches away from the bat's line), while CUT3R follows
  the thin diagonal more faithfully, if imperfectly.

**Revised conclusion:** CUT3R-trained's failure mode is consistent and
one-directional across this whole set — confident over-prediction into
background clutter, every time it loses badly. DINOv2's failures are not
one thing: on apple it mislocates outright, on microwave/bench it
under-covers, and on both baseballbat windows it produces the same kind of
scattered noise CUT3R shows elsewhere. So "DINOv2 is the clean, careful
backbone" is only true on average (matching its balanced global
precision/recall) — on individual hard windows it fails in several different
ways, while CUT3R-trained's individual failures are all the same failure.
