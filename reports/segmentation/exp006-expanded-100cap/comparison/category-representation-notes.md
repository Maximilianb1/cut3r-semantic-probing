# Does training-data volume per category explain per-category difficulty?

Short check, generated by
`python -m src.segmentation.analysis.build_category_representation_check` (run against
the expanded-training results). Figure: `category-representation-check.png`
in this same folder.

## Setup

Per-category train-window counts, from the real combined train cache used
for expanded training (original + leftover + cap100-new-train, 10,029
windows / 26 categories), plotted against each backbone's per-category test
foreground IoU.

## Result

| Backbone | Pearson r | Spearman r |
|---|---:|---:|
| CUT3R-trained | +0.633 | +0.177 |
| CUT3R-random | +0.176 | +0.104 |
| DINOv2 | +0.533 | +0.334 |

The Pearson numbers look like a real relationship. They are not — look at the
figure: 25 of 26 categories sit in a narrow band of ~370-400 train windows
(the cap100 expansion made category representation nearly uniform), and only
`parkingmeter` sits apart at ~181 windows. That single point is also the
worst- or near-worst-scoring category for every backbone, so it alone drags
the Pearson correlation up by leverage — one point at low-x/low-y makes a
linear fit look meaningful when the bulk of the data says otherwise.

**Look instead at the dense cluster itself** (the ~370-400-window band, i.e.
almost every category): at essentially *constant* training volume, test IoU
still spans the *entire* range — from near 0.0 to near 0.98 for CUT3R-random,
0.48 to 0.98 for CUT3R-trained, 0.6 to 0.95 for DINOv2. Spearman (rank
correlation, far less sensitive to one outlier) reflects this honestly: weak
for all three backbones (+0.10 to +0.33).

## What this teaches

**Training-data volume per category is not the main driver of per-category
difficulty**, at least not in the range this project can produce (the
cap100 expansion already nearly equalizes categories). Categories that score
badly (parkingmeter, toilet, cup) are not badly represented in training —
most of them have as many training windows as categories that score
excellently (teddybear, umbrella, plant). This is consistent with, and
reinforces, the qualitative finding elsewhere in this record: per-category
(and per-window) failures track the object's visual properties — small
size, thin shape, cluttered background — not how many times the model saw
that category during training.

**Practical implication:** if a category like parkingmeter or toilet is
still weak after Pillar B's 3.3x data increase, throwing still more training
videos at it is unlikely to be the fix (representation was already
sufficient for other, better-scoring categories at a similar count). The
more promising lever is the head/loss side (`pos_weight`, since these are
exactly the low-foreground-fraction categories) rather than further data
collection — consistent with the plan's own prioritization of loss
weighting before more data or a bigger head.

**One partial exception:** parkingmeter's genuinely low window count (about
half the typical category) means it cannot be fully ruled out as
*also* a data-volume story for CUT3R-trained/CUT3R-random specifically
(both score poorly there). But DINOv2 scores respectably on the same
low-count category (~0.51), which argues against volume being the primary
explanation even for this one exception — a representation that can extract
signal from parkingmeter's ~181 windows exists (DINOv2 does), so the gap is
more plausibly about what CUT3R's features encode for that category than
about how many windows it had.
